# DrivePerf: Android enthusiast, telemetry and PDR evidence

Research date: 17 September 2026. Supplement to the Android market research; incorporates the clarified audience of enthusiasts and drivers seeking coaching. This is evidence and research direction, not a feature specification.

## What this changes

DrivePerf should evaluate two related jobs: understanding a drive in detail, and knowing what to improve next. Enthusiasts are not necessarily track drivers: a person may want rich statistics about ordinary road trips. Racing analytics are a separate context within the enthusiast audience. Insurance programs are a source of measurement and interaction lessons, not the product's initial customer proposition.

The enthusiast market already has substantial data capture, graphs, video, exports and lap comparison. Some newcomers advertise automated coaching. A modern appearance alone is therefore an unvalidated differentiator. A stronger hypothesis is understandable analysis that scales from an ordinary trip to deeper telemetry, with visible measurement quality and coaching appropriate to the driving context.

## Evidence boundaries

- Official Google Play listings and developer documentation were inspected; no app was installed or road/track tested. A listing verifies a public Android product listing, not availability for every Canadian account, device or OS version.
- Prices are snapshots of the visible US Play storefront or the developer's stated USD price. Taxes, Canadian prices, subscription amounts and hardware costs require confirmation during hands-on testing.
- Features below are developer claims. Advertising such as dyno accuracy or AI coaching is not independent validation.
- Reviews are selected, dated examples visible on the Android listing. They identify hypotheses, not prevalence, defect rates or current-version diagnoses. Edited reviews may contain older incidents; developer replies are identified separately.
- A missing mention in a listing does not establish that a feature is absent. Android/iOS feature parity is not assumed.

## Cosworth PDR: future-direction comparator

Use accurate product identities. **AliveDrive** is Cosworth's recording system, branded **Performance Data Recorder (PDR)** in GM vehicles and **Drive eXperience Recorder (DXR)** in Dodge vehicles. It combines in-vehicle video/audio and GPS telemetry with driver inputs and vehicle dynamics. Its analysis ecosystem includes **Cosworth Toolbox** (Windows, for supported older PDR generations), **AliveDrive Desktop** (Windows/macOS, including newer PDR generations), and **AliveDrive Race** (Android/iOS). Cosworth also links **Pi Toolbox**, its broader motorsport analysis product. Compatibility depends on vehicle/model year and recorder generation; these names are not interchangeable. [Cosworth analysis-suite overview](https://www.cosworth.com/motorsport/support/alivedrive-data-analysis-tools/).

The [verified Android AliveDrive Race listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.cosworth.alivedrive.race.phone.droid&hl=en_US) is a companion to that ecosystem. It should be catalogued separately from apps that record a trip using the phone's sensors. The [Cosworth Toolbox product page](https://www.cosworth.com/motorsport/products/cosworth-toolbox/) describes synchronized vehicle data and video analysis for drivers and engineers.

**Future research hypothesis:** DrivePerf could eventually support the same high-level cycle—record, align data with places/events, compare, explain and practice—while remaining a phone-first product. Investigate optional supported imports or hardware integrations only when user demand and format access justify them. No PDR compatibility, OEM partnership, equivalent accuracy or replacement claim is implied.

## Candidate metric tiers to investigate

These are research groupings, not paid tiers or committed specifications.

| Candidate group | Plausible outputs | Source and validity questions |
|---|---|---|
| **Everyday trip understanding** | Route, duration, distance, speed distribution, stopped time, repeat-trip comparison and recording completeness. | Location timestamps, speed/position and their reported uncertainty; define moving-time logic and detect gaps. Android exposes optional accuracy estimates—missing estimates must remain missing. [Android Location API](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/location/Location). |
| **Motion and smoothness analysis** | Longitudinal/lateral acceleration, deceleration episodes, jerk, turn intensity and consistency. | Accelerometer, gyroscope and orientation; compensate gravity and calibrate phone-to-vehicle axes. A moving phone can invalidate a vehicle-motion interpretation. Android distinguishes sensor axes and gravity-inclusive versus linear acceleration. [Android motion sensors](https://developer.android.com/develop/sensors-and-location/sensors/sensors_motion). |
| **Coaching and efficient-driving proxies** | One or two actionable observations, trends, smoothness consistency and stop/start patterns. | Inference requiring a validated baseline and context. Smoothness is not proof of safety, and a fuel-efficiency proxy is not measured fuel or battery consumption. Do not infer correct braking decisions from acceleration alone. |
| **Closed-course performance** | Laps/sectors, time delta, repeatability, speed traces, G plots and video alignment. | Validate phone-specific performance first; add higher-rate external GNSS where measurement needs warrant it. A vendor recommends 5 Hz minimum and 10 Hz+ for motorsports, but that is its guidance, not a universal engineering guarantee. [Petrel GPS guidance](https://www.petreldata.com/gps-for-motorsports/). |
| **Vehicle telemetry / specialist coaching** | Throttle, RPM, gear, braking input, steering, energy/fuel and vehicle temperatures where available. | Require supported external/OEM data. Generic OBD support does not guarantee steering angle, brake pressure, desired sampling rate or model-specific channels. [Petrel OBD/CAN explanation](https://www.petreldata.com/obd-ii-and-can-bus-extracting-performance-data-for-motorsports/). |

A phone directly observes its own movement; it does not directly measure brake pedal pressure, throttle position, steering angle, tire temperature, traction-control intervention or consumed fuel. Some vehicle quantities may be **modeled** using additional inputs, as PerfExpert illustrates. Label provenance as observed, derived, modeled or unavailable; a missing channel must never appear as a measured zero.

The same deceleration can mean avoidable abruptness in a routine trip, necessary hazard avoidance, or intentional braking on a circuit. A single universal “good driving” score would erase that context. Research separate road/coaching and closed-course interpretations, while letting everyday enthusiasts inspect richer data without entering a racing workflow. On-road comparisons should evaluate consistency and appropriate smoothness rather than reward shortest elapsed time.

## UI and validation opportunities

These are hypotheses inferred from the evidence, not claims that every competitor lacks them:

1. **Explain the event before the score.** Show the relevant trace, its location, confidence and one useful interpretation. Let the user inspect a detailed layer without requiring every beginner to understand it.
2. **Make recording trust visible.** Expose mount/calibration readiness, GPS quality, sample gaps, connected-device health and whether the session has been durably saved. Test partial-session recovery and phone migration.
3. **Keep expert evidence accessible.** Synchronize map, timeline and graphs; include units and data-source identity. Offer export with timestamps and metadata, not just a shareable image.
4. **Use progressive information density.** Compare a short coach summary with an enthusiast dashboard; test comprehension, large fonts, color-independent chart meaning and interaction cost.
5. **Connect evidence to practice.** Compare equivalent trips or controlled sessions, give a focused improvement goal, and test whether the next session demonstrates progress. “More statistics” and AI-written advice are not themselves proof of useful coaching.
6. **Validate measurement before promotion.** Benchmark several Android tiers, mounting positions, poor reception, background recording, Bluetooth interruption, battery/thermal limits and video load. Use repeatable closed-course reference sessions for timing/G claims; use appropriate vehicle measurements for energy claims.

Suggested next research activities: interview ordinary-road enthusiasts separately from track-day participants; test the same recording/review/export tasks in RaceChrono, Lap Legend and an everyday driving app; observe novice interpretation of a shared sample trip; then conduct a small sensor-quality pilot. Carry the Cosworth comparison into the future roadmap as an analysis and coaching ambition, not an initial delivery requirement.
